Surfaces are separated by **borders**; shadow means a surface genuinely floats above the page.
There is no single named overlay role any more — a floating surface reaches for Tailwind's own
`shadow-sm`, `shadow-md` or `shadow-lg`, at the weight upstream gives that component.

<ShadowScale />

## Where shadow appears

- **`shadow-md`** — popovers, menus and selects.
- **`shadow-lg`** — sheets and submenus.
- **`shadow-sm`** — the active tab chip, the floating sidebar, the inset main area.
- **none** — cards, panels, rows, inputs and resting controls, which separate with `border-border`.

Sticky surfaces gain a separating border when they detach from adjacent content; they do not
acquire an invented elevation tier.

## Equal-elevation nesting

Do not nest a bordered card inside another bordered card at the same visual elevation. Group the
content with spacing, a divider, or a deliberately different surface role. When a nested region is
necessary, its border/radius must communicate hierarchy rather than duplicate the outer container.

## Stacking

There is **one portal band**, `z-50`, and DOM order decides nesting within it — `z-10` remains for a
local raise inside a component. The named `--z-raised` / `--z-overlay` / `--z-toast` tokens are
gone: OVL-2 is decided as shadcn, and upstream writes `z-50` on every portaled surface.

That has one user-visible consequence worth knowing before you hit it. **A toast fired from inside
an open dialog renders behind the scrim.** The toast viewport mounts with the app provider, before
any dialog opens, so on DOM order the later dialog wins. The system used to buy its way out with a
`z-60` toast band; it no longer does, because upstream does not. Raise the toast after the dialog
closes, or mount your toaster after it. `test/stacking.browser.test.tsx` measures the behaviour
rather than assuming it.
