Surfaces are separated by **borders**; shadow means a surface genuinely floats above the page.
There is no single named overlay role any more — a floating surface reaches for Tailwind's own
`shadow-sm`, `shadow-md` or `shadow-lg`, at the weight upstream gives that component.

<ShadowScale />

## Where shadow appears

- **`shadow-md`** — popovers, menus and selects.
- **`shadow-lg`** — sheets and submenus.
- **`shadow-sm`** — the active tab chip, the floating sidebar, the inset main area.
- **none** — cards, panels, rows, inputs and resting controls, which separate with `border-border`
  (floating surfaces draw the same border under their shadow — see below).

Sticky surfaces gain a separating border when they detach from adjacent content; they do not
acquire an invented elevation tier.

## Borders, not rings

Every card and every floating surface — dialog, alert dialog, popover, hover card, select,
combobox, the three menus and the navigation-menu panel — draws a real 1px `border border-border`.
None of them carries a `ring-*` outline. Shadow is the only thing that changes between a resting
card and a floating popover; the edge is the same hairline on both.

Upstream shadcn draws that edge as `ring-1 ring-foreground/10`, a box-shadow. The system took it
at the reset and reversed it (decision **BRD-1**, 23-09-2026), for three reasons:

- **It read as a stray outline.** A foreground-tinted ring sits outside the box and looks like a
  focus or selection state that nobody asked for, not like the surface's own edge.
- **It vanishes in forced colours.** Windows High Contrast drops box-shadows, so a ring-edged
  surface lost its boundary entirely; a border is repainted in the system colour.
- **It contradicted the rest of the system.** Inputs, tables, alerts and settings rows already
  separate with `border-border`; one edge vocabulary is easier to hold than two.

The border takes a pixel of layout on each side and sits inside the radius, so a card's
`overflow-hidden` clips a full-bleed image at the border's inner edge and the corners stay
concentric. The avatar group's `ring-2 ring-background` is not an outline — it is the gap that
separates stacked avatars — and stays a ring.

## Equal-elevation nesting

Do not nest a bordered card inside another bordered card at the same visual elevation. Group the
content with spacing, a divider, or a deliberately different surface role. When a nested region is
necessary, its border/radius must communicate hierarchy rather than duplicate the outer container.

## Stacking

There is **one portal band**, `z-50`, and DOM order decides nesting within it — `z-10` remains for a
local raise inside a component. The named `--z-raised` / `--z-overlay` / `--z-toast` tokens are
gone: OVL-2 is decided as shadcn, and upstream writes `z-50` on every portaled surface.

**The one exception is the Toast viewport, which sits at `z-60`** (decision **OVL-15**, since
0.12.0). DOM order cannot serve it: the viewport mounts with the app provider, before any dialog
exists, so on DOM order alone a toast fired from an open dialog would render behind the scrim. One
band above `z-50` puts it in front of every overlay instead. Nothing else leaves `z-50` — do not
copy `z-60` onto a surface of your own. `test/stacking.browser.test.tsx` measures the behaviour
rather than assuming it.
