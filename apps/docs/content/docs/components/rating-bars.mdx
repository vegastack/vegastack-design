## Usage

```tsx
import { RatingBars } from "@/components/ui/rating-bars";

<div className="flex flex-col gap-1.5">
  <RatingBars label="Speed" value={4} valueText="Fast" />
  <RatingBars label="Cost" value={2} valueText="Low" />
  <RatingBars label="Quality" value={5} valueText="Strongest" />
</div>;
```

`RatingBars` shows a rating on a small fixed scale at a glance — how fast, how costly, how strong —
as a label, a row of segments of which the first `value` are lit, and a word after them. The word
carries the meaning; the segments let a stack of ratings be compared down the column. Every row puts
its label in the same 4rem column, so stacked rows line up without a wrapper. Lit segments take the
`primary` fill and unlit ones the `muted` track, the pair `Progress` uses, in light and dark. It
renders on the server and has no motion.

`RatingBars` also takes the `<div>` element's own props (`className`, `ref`, `title`, …) except
`children`; the root is the meter. `value` is clamped to `0`–`max` and lights the nearest whole number of segments; `max` is a whole
number of at least 1.

## Accessibility

- The root is `role="meter"`, named by `label`, with `aria-valuenow` (the clamped value, `0`
  for `null`), `aria-valuemin` `0`, `aria-valuemax` `max` and `aria-valuetext` `valueText` — a screen
  reader hears "Speed, Fast", not a number.
- The segments are `aria-hidden`: they draw the value the meter already states.
- Nothing in it is focusable or interactive.

## Do / Don't

<DoDont
  do="Give every row a word that says what the rating means (Fast, Low, Strongest, Not listed)."
  dont="Rely on the lit count alone, or show a raw number or a price in valueText."
/>

<DoDont
  do="Stack rows with the same max so they compare down the column."
  dont="Use it for live or continuous values — a level, a progress — where Progress or LevelMeter fits."
/>
