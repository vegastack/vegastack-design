## Usage

```tsx
import { VideoPlayer } from "@/components/ui/video-player";

<VideoPlayer
  src="/media/demo.mp4"
  poster="/media/demo-poster.webp"
  label="Product demo video"
/>;
```

## Examples

### Basic

The video surface owns a hover-revealed overlay transport: a thin media progress rail that expands
smoothly on hover or focus, then play/pause, time, volume, settings, and fullscreen in the lower row.
`poster` shows in the frame until playback starts — store one at upload with `probeVideo` from the
`media-probe` lib — and the player never autoplays. Settings holds playback speed; a Quality entry
appears only when you pass `qualityOptions` (and switch renditions in `onQualityChange`), since a
single stored file has one. The docs preview uses a 90-second local MP4 fixture, so it can play
without network access.

### One at a time

Starting the video pauses any other DS player that is playing — an `AudioPlayer`, the global
player, the `FileViewer` stage — and starting one of them pauses the video. Nothing resumes by
itself. `exclusive={false}` opts it out both ways.

### Aspect ratio

`aspectRatio="video"` reserves a 16:9 frame. Use `square` for square media or `auto`
when the video element should keep its intrinsic height.

### Can't play

A browser cannot play every video: an HEVC `.mov` in Chrome or Firefox, an `.mkv`, an `.avi`. On a
media error the frame shows "Can’t play this video here" (`loadErrorLabel`) with a Download button
to `downloadHref`, and the transport — which would do nothing — goes away. Without `downloadHref`
the message shows alone.

### Expired URLs

A signed URL expires; a video paused past its lifetime fails on the next seek. Pass
`onSourceExpired` and, on the first media error, the player calls it once, loads the URL it
resolves and resumes at the same position — playing, if it was. Returning the same URL (a route
that re-signs on every request) reloads it. A second failure shows the "can't play" card. This is
the same contract as [`AudioPlayer`](/docs/components/audio-player)'s `onSourceExpired`.

```tsx
<VideoPlayer
  src={`/api/files/${file.id}`}
  poster={file.posterUrl}
  label={file.name}
  downloadHref={`/api/files/${file.id}?download`}
  onSourceExpired={async () => `/api/files/${file.id}`}
/>
```

### HLS playlists

Pass `hls` instead of `src` for a recording served as segments. Safari on macOS and iOS plays the
playlist natively; everywhere else the player loads hls.js with a dynamic `import()` the first time
it attaches, so a page that never plays a playlist never downloads it. `attach` decides when that
is: once half the player is on screen (`visible`, the default) or on the first pointer or keyboard
interaction (`interaction`); the poster shows until then. Pass `hlsWorkerPath` — a same-origin copy
of hls.js's `dist/hls.worker.js` — so transmuxing runs in a worker under `worker-src 'self'`.

A signed playlist URL expires. `hls.onExpired` resolves a fresh one: the player calls it 60 seconds
before `expiresAt`, and when the playlist or a segment answers 403, then reloads the new playlist
at the same position, playing if it was. Re-render with the new `expiresAt` after a renewal. A
second 403 before the renewed playlist has loaded, or a rejection, shows the "can't play" card.
The `src` and `onSourceExpired` contract for files is unchanged.

### Chapters

`chapters` (`start_ms` on the media's own clock) draws a small break in the scrubber at each
chapter start, names the chapter over the rail while the pointer hovers it, and adds a Chapters
entry to the settings menu that jumps to a chapter.

### Markers

`markers` (`at_ms` on the media's own clock, and a `label`) marks moments people pointed at, such as
comments made at a time: a small dot above the scrubber at each one, and its label over the rail
when the pointer is near it. Markers are never targets of their own: the moment's own link, where
it's listed, plays from there.

### Captions

`captions` adds one WebVTT track and a captions control (and <kbd>C</kbd>). Captions start off; the
viewer's choice is remembered on the device. The video then loads with `crossOrigin="anonymous"`,
so the server that hosts the track (and the video) must send CORS headers.

### Skip ranges

`skipRanges` previews an edit before it is rendered: `[from, to]` pairs in milliseconds of the
media's own clock. Playback jumps past each range (on `timeupdate` and, where the browser has it,
on every frame through `requestVideoFrameCallback`), and the scrubber, the readout, chapters and the
0–9 jumps all use the edited time, so the duration matches the rendered version. `onTimeUpdate`
and `controllerRef` stay on the media's own clock, so a transcript can follow along with
timestamps it already has.

### Picture in picture and fullscreen

Where the browser supports it, a picture-in-picture control (and <kbd>P</kbd>) moves the video into
a floating window (`requestPictureInPicture`, or Safari's presentation mode). Fullscreen takes the
whole frame, controls included; iOS Safari, which can only fullscreen the video element, opens its
system player.

### Following playback

`onTimeUpdate(ms)` reports the position as it moves. `controllerRef` exposes `seekTo(ms)`,
`play()`, `pause()` and `currentMs()` for a transcript that seeks the video when a line is clicked.

### Controls always visible

The overlay reveals on pointer or focus and fades a second after the pointer leaves; while the video
plays it also steps aside after 2.5 seconds without the pointer moving, and once it has played and
is paused it stays up. `controlsVisible`
takes that out of the loop — `true` pins the chrome open, `false` keeps it closed. Use it for a
kiosk or always-on player, and for a static fixture: the auto-reveal never fires without a pointer,
so a screenshot or a contract check would otherwise capture a frame with no controls in it.

## Accessibility

| Key                                 | Action                                                                     |
| ----------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| <kbd>Tab</kbd>                      | Move through play/pause, seek, volume, captions, settings, PiP, fullscreen |
| <kbd>Enter</kbd> / <kbd>Space</kbd> | Activate the focused button                                                |
| <kbd>Space</kbd>                    | Play or pause when the player is focused                                   |
| <kbd>K</kbd>                        | Play or pause                                                              |
| <kbd>←</kbd> / <kbd>→</kbd>         | Back or forward `arrowSkipSeconds` (default `skipSeconds`) when focused    |
| <kbd>J</kbd> / <kbd>L</kbd>         | Back or forward `skipSeconds` (default 15)                                 |
| <kbd>0</kbd>–<kbd>9</kbd>           | Jump to 0–90 % of the video                                                |
| <kbd>&lt;</kbd> / <kbd>&gt;</kbd>   | Slower or faster (Shift+, and Shift+.)                                     |
| <kbd>↑</kbd> / <kbd>↓</kbd>         | Volume up or down when the player is focused                               |
| <kbd>M</kbd>                        | Toggle mute                                                                |
| <kbd>C</kbd>                        | Toggle captions, when there are captions                                   |
| <kbd>P</kbd>                        | Toggle picture in picture, where the browser supports it                   |
| <kbd>F</kbd>                        | Toggle fullscreen when the player is focused                               |
| <kbd>Home</kbd> / <kbd>End</kbd>    | Seek to the beginning or the end while the scrubber is focused             |

- The scrubber says where it is as "1:23 of 4:56" (`aria-valuetext`), on the edited timeline when
  there are skip ranges.
- Pass a descriptive `label`; it names the video element and prefixes every custom control.
- The named media progress rail composes `Slider`, so keyboard and screen-reader slider semantics
  stay intact while its video-specific hover treatment remains separate from the generic component.
- Icon-only transport buttons are `Button size="icon"`, each carrying its own `aria-label`.
- Tooltips expose the primary keyboard shortcuts for pointer and keyboard users.
- The volume slider is exposed from the mute control and keeps slider keyboard semantics.
- Playback speed (and quality, when `qualityOptions` are passed) live in settings submenus with
  radio selection.
- A video that cannot play announces "Can’t play this video here" as an alert, and its Download
  button is a real link with the `download` attribute.
- Rewind and forward are keyboard shortcuts rather than visible buttons.
- The captions and picture-in-picture controls are toggle buttons (`aria-pressed`) and appear only
  when there are captions, or the browser has picture in picture.
- Waits longer than 400 ms show the loading bolt in the middle of the frame.
- The overlay controls appear on hover or focus and fade out after the pointer leaves the frame.
- The fullscreen action changes to an exit-fullscreen icon and accessible name while the frame owns
  document fullscreen. The transport's tooltips and its settings menu stay visible there: they
  portal into the fullscreen element rather than to `<body>`, which is decision OVL-14. See
  [`MediaPlayerControls`](/docs/components/media-player-controls) § Accessibility.
- The native `<video>` element is the media engine and visual frame; browser-native controls are
  omitted so audio and video players share the same VegaStack transport.
