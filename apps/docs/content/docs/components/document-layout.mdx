## Usage

```tsx
import {
  DocumentLayout,
  DocumentLayoutBody,
  DocumentLayoutContents,
  DocumentLayoutFoot,
  DocumentLayoutHeader,
  DocumentLayoutIcon,
  DocumentLayoutMargin,
  DocumentLayoutTitle,
  DocumentLayoutTitleGroup,
} from "@/components/ui/document-layout";

<DocumentLayout margin={threadCount > 0 || drafting}>
  <DocumentLayoutHeader>
    <DocumentLayoutTitleGroup>
      <DocumentLayoutIcon>📘</DocumentLayoutIcon>
      <DocumentLayoutTitle>Pricing v2</DocumentLayoutTitle>
    </DocumentLayoutTitleGroup>
    <MetaLine>
      <MetaLineItem>Updated 3m ago</MetaLineItem>
    </MetaLine>
  </DocumentLayoutHeader>
  {outline.length > 1 ? (
    <DocumentLayoutContents>
      <TableOfContents
        variant="rail"
        side="start"
        sticky={false}
        items={outline}
      />
    </DocumentLayoutContents>
  ) : null}
  <DocumentLayoutBody>
    <MarkdownView typeScale="document">{markdown}</MarkdownView>
  </DocumentLayoutBody>
  <DocumentLayoutFoot>{pagesInside}</DocumentLayoutFoot>
  <DocumentLayoutMargin>{threads}</DocumentLayoutMargin>
</DocumentLayout>;
```

`DocumentLayout` is the frame of a page people read and write as a document — a wiki page, a
spec, its public view and a version's full view all wear the same one. The text column is fluid:
about three quarters of the layout's width, between bounds that grow with each tier, so a large
monitor gets a generous column rather than a narrow strip. The title area sits above the text and
what follows it — sub-pages, the page's own comments — below, in the same column. Before the text,
in its gutter, a contents rail; after it, when there is room, a comment margin.

The layout follows its **own width**, not the window's (container queries in rem), so a collapsed
sidebar or a split view lands in the right tier:

| Tier | Width     | Gutters | Text                     | Contents                 | Comments                    |
| ---- | --------- | ------- | ------------------------ | ------------------------ | --------------------------- |
| S    | < 40rem   | 0       | the full width           | the host's menu or sheet | the host's sheet or popover |
| M    | 40–64rem  | 3rem    | max(36rem, 75%)          | the rail, in the gutter  | a popover at the highlight  |
| L    | 64–90rem  | 3rem    | clamp(40rem, 75%, 52rem) | the rail, in the gutter  | the margin (20rem)          |
| XL   | 90–120rem | 4rem    | 60rem                    | the rail, in the gutter  | the margin (24rem)          |
| 2XL  | ≥ 120rem  | 4rem    | 64rem                    | the rail, in the gutter  | the margin (24rem)          |

At the document type scale (14px Geist) that is about 94 characters a line at M's 36rem, 128 at
L's 52rem, 150 at XL and 161 at 2XL; the 24px line height carries the longer lines.

The tier bounds are the system's layout tiers — `tablet` 40rem, `laptop` 64rem, `wide` 90rem
(`LAYOUT_TIERS` in `page-layout`) — plus a document-only 120rem step.

The rail, the text and the margin are one block, centred, and the text **stays in the middle when
the comment margin shows**: the start side widens to mirror the margin, so wherever the width can
hold the margin on both sides the text sits exactly where it sat without comments. With less room
the start side gives way first, and the margin shifts the block towards the start only by what is
missing; the text narrows only when the width cannot hold the text, the margin and the gutters at
once.

It is one subtree at every width: a column with no room is `display: none`, never unmounted, so
an editor inside never remounts when the window resizes. `margin` shows the comment column from
L up; set it only while the column holds something (open threads beside the text, or a comment
being written), decided with the text so the first paint is right. A page's first comment keeps
its text in place while there is room for the margin on both sides.
From M up the grid keeps its start gutter whether or not a page has headings, and
`DocumentLayoutContents` sits in it, stacked above the text and the margin: a
`TableOfContents variant="rail" side="start"` there is a slim column of dashes that opens into the
labelled list over the text — so headings that arrive (or go) never move the text, and the open
list takes the pointer wherever it lies. Put the parts in reading order — header, contents, body,
foot, margin. Server-safe: the tiers are CSS.

## Examples

### Wide

`measure="wide"` gives the text the whole width less the gutters (and the margin, where it
shows), up to 75rem (1200px), past which a line is too long to read — each reader's choice,
never the document's.

### Aside

`aside` gives the layout a rail after the text: `"discussion"` (the comments) or `"outline"` (the
contents), and `null` while it is closed. A layout with an `aside` — even `null` — keeps the end
track for it from the L tier, so opening or closing the rail never moves or narrows the text; in
`wide` only an open aside takes its width (wide is 1200px less the open aside). Put a
`DocumentLayoutAside` of each kind in the layout and the one `aside` names shows:

- **Discussion** — a non-modal rail as tall as the window: the heading with its `count`, a ×
  (`onClose`), and `tabs` such as Open · Resolved · Page, plus Review while a review is open, each
  with an optional count. The page stays readable and editable beside it. It takes the comment
  margin's place, so pass `CommentMargin quote={false}` for the cards beside the text and keep the
  quote in the rail. Below the L tier it is a bottom sheet instead, the whole screen below 640px.
- **Outline** — the contents list, pinned from the XL tier while the discussion is closed; below XL
  the contents rail's hover ticks stay.

Leave `aside` unset and nothing changes: the margin and the rail behave as before.

### Title

`DocumentLayoutTitle` is the document type scale's top step: 30/36 semibold in the heading face,
one step above the text's `#` (24/32). `DocumentLayoutTitleGroup` holds an optional
`DocumentLayoutIcon` over it, 4px apart: an emoji at the title's size, or the control that changes
it. `reveal` hides an "Add icon" control until the title group is hovered or holds focus (always
shown on a coarse pointer). The row is 32px whatever it holds, so showing, adding or removing
an icon moves nothing.

```tsx
<DocumentLayoutTitleGroup>
  <DocumentLayoutIcon reveal>
    <Button variant="ghost" size="xs">
      <SmilePlus aria-hidden />
      Add icon
    </Button>
  </DocumentLayoutIcon>
  <DocumentLayoutTitle>Pricing v2</DocumentLayoutTitle>
</DocumentLayoutTitleGroup>
```

### Meta lines

`DocumentLayoutMeta` is a line of small facts under the title — who updated the page, where it came
from, what links to it. Each `DocumentLayoutMetaItem` opens with a `·`, and the row is drawn one
dot's width to the start of a clipped box, so a wrapped line never starts (or the line before it
ends) with a stray dot. An item takes an `icon`, and `nowrap` keeps a fact built around a link on one line (the link
truncates); `DocumentLayoutMetaButton` is a fact that opens
something ("Used in 2 tasks") and `DocumentLayoutMetaLink` a fact's link (`render` takes the
router's link).

`variant="status"` is the status row: one reserved line from `sm` and two on a phone, from the
first paint, so chips that stream in move nothing. Group its facts: a `DocumentLayoutMetaGroup`
(`kind="unit"`) stays together — a `DocumentLayoutMetaField` ("Owner Sanjay") and its chip, wrapping
inside only when even the chip's short form doesn't fit — and a
`kind="flow"` group of flags flows with the row from `sm`; on a phone each group is a whole line.
A `DocumentLayoutMetaBadge` with `label` and `short` keeps all its words while the row has room;
on a tight row the chips drop to their short forms, the longest first (the whole label stays in
the chip's tooltip), and only if those don't fit either does the row wrap. Pass what the chips say
as `watch`, and `DocumentLayoutMetaSkeleton` while they stream in.

```tsx
<DocumentLayoutMeta variant="status" watch={[status, review]}>
  <DocumentLayoutMetaGroup>
    <DocumentLayoutMetaField label="Owner">
      Sanjay Desai
    </DocumentLayoutMetaField>
    <DocumentLayoutMetaBadge
      variant="success"
      label="Verified · Oct 14"
      short="Verified"
    >
      <BadgeCheck data-icon="inline-start" aria-hidden />
    </DocumentLayoutMetaBadge>
  </DocumentLayoutMetaGroup>
  <DocumentLayoutMetaGroup kind="flow">
    <DocumentLayoutMetaItem>
      <DocumentLayoutMetaBadge variant="warning">
        2 out of date
      </DocumentLayoutMetaBadge>
    </DocumentLayoutMetaItem>
  </DocumentLayoutMetaGroup>
</DocumentLayoutMeta>
```

Where the contents rail has no room (the S tier), `DocumentLayoutContentsButton` is a round "On
this page" button floating at the window's bottom end — give it to `TableOfContents` as its
`trigger`. `DocumentLayoutTitle` takes `editable` when the title renames in place (the text cursor
over the whole row).

### Page tools

The tools a page lays over or around its text, each without a class of your own:

- `DocumentLayoutFindBar` — find in the text: a search field docked at the top of the text on a
  band of the page's background, with where the find stands ("3 of 12") and Previous, Next and
  Close (Enter, Shift+Enter, Escape). The host finds the matches; pass `highlights` (two CSS
  Custom Highlight names) and the bar paints them.
- `DocumentLayoutJumpCue` — "2 new comments ↓", a pill pinned to the bottom of the view while
  something new is out of sight; it takes no room.
- `DocumentLayoutFloatingButton` — a phone's one floating action ("Edit"), at the window's bottom
  end, clear of the home indicator.
- `DocumentLayoutStarters` with `DocumentLayoutStarter` pills — ways to start an empty page
  ("Start from: Meeting notes · Spec · More templates…"; `more` for the quiet last one).
- `DocumentLayoutNote` — a quiet line under the text.
- `DocumentLayoutDecisionBar` with `DocumentLayoutDecisionBarText` and
  `DocumentLayoutDecisionBarActions` — a decision the reader owes the page ("Asha asked you to
  review this page · Approve"). Put it first in `DocumentLayoutBody`: it sticks under the top bar
  while the text is in view, and on a phone floats at the bottom with the floating buttons above it.
- `DocumentLayout focusable` — the canvas takes focus from a click in its read text (never a tab
  stop) and shows no focus cue of its own.

### Sections, pages and live blocks

What a document holds besides its prose, again without a class of your own:

- `DocumentLayoutSection` — a section at the foot ("Pages inside"), named by its
  `DocumentLayoutSectionTitle` (`count` follows it in the muted ink: "Pages inside · 3"), its title
  row a `DocumentLayoutSectionHeader` with an action at the end. `DocumentLayoutSectionButton` is
  its quiet button: `look="more"` loads the rest ("Show more pages", "Load earlier"), `look="add"`
  is the one line an empty section shows to someone who can fill it. `DocumentLayoutSectionSkeleton`
  holds its place while it loads.
- `DocumentLayoutPageList` with `DocumentLayoutPageItem` rows — the pages under a document, ruled
  above, between and below. `DocumentLayoutPageLink` is a row's target: its emoji (`icon`, a page
  icon without one), name, one-line `excerpt` and `meta`, the whole row the link; a ⋯ menu follows
  it in the item.
- `DocumentLayoutBlock` — a live block drawn into the text (a task list): a card with a
  `DocumentLayoutBlockHeader` (`DocumentLayoutBlockTitle`, a ghost button at the end), then
  `DocumentLayoutBlockRows` of `DocumentLayoutBlockRow`s (`DocumentLayoutBlockRowMain` with a
  `DocumentLayoutBlockRowRef`, then `DocumentLayoutBlockRowMeta`, which wraps under the main line
  below 640px), or a `DocumentLayoutBlockNote` ("No tasks match") or a
  `DocumentLayoutBlockSkeleton`, and a `DocumentLayoutBlockFooter`. `DocumentLayoutBlockAction` is
  the link-styled action inside it (Try again, Open in Tasks →). A block of a table's records
  shows them as a `DocumentLayoutBlockTable` (`columns`, the name column first): a
  `DocumentLayoutBlockTableHead` row of `DocumentLayoutBlockTableColumn`s, then a
  `DocumentLayoutBlockTableBody` of `DocumentLayoutBlockTableRow`s of
  `DocumentLayoutBlockTableCell`s (`primary` on the name column's, a row header). It is read-only,
  every row's columns line up, and it scrolls sideways when its columns don't fit.

### Skeleton

`DocumentLayoutSkeleton` is the page's loading state on the SAME grid and tier — an icon row
(`icon`), a title line, `metaLines` meta lines (default 1) and paragraphs of text bars at the
document line pitch (a 14px bar every 24px, 16 every 26 on a coarse pointer), 12px between
paragraphs — so nothing moves when the page arrives. Pass it the `margin`, `measure`, `icon` and
`metaLines` the page will have. It is one `status` region, named by `label`.

### The tier in script

Branches CSS cannot take — comments in the margin or in a popover, the contents on the rail or
in a menu — read the tier with `useDocumentLayoutTier(ref)`. It reads `--document-layout-tier`, which
the container queries set, so script and stylesheet never disagree about where a tier starts.
`fallback` (default `"xl"`) is the answer on the server and before the first measurement.

```tsx
import { useDocumentLayoutTier } from "@/components/ui/document-layout";

const ref = React.useRef<HTMLDivElement>(null);
const tier = useDocumentLayoutTier(ref);

<DocumentLayout ref={ref} margin>
  …
  {tier !== "s" && tier !== "m" ? (
    <DocumentLayoutMargin>{threads}</DocumentLayoutMargin>
  ) : null}
</DocumentLayout>;
```

### Print

A document prints as one column of its header, body and foot — the contents and the comment margin
drop out — on a named page with a 16mm × 14mm paper margin, in the light palette whatever the
reader's theme. With `typeScale="document"`, `MarkdownView` and `TextEdit` add the text's own
print rules: code wraps, tables print in full, highlights and controls drop out, and an external
link prints its address.

`DocumentLayoutHeader`, `DocumentLayoutTitleGroup`, `DocumentLayoutBody` and `DocumentLayoutFoot`
take a `div`'s native props, `DocumentLayoutTitle` an `h1`'s; `DocumentLayoutContents` and `DocumentLayoutMargin` take an `aside`'s. What the contents
hold sticks `--document-layout-sticky-top` (default `--spacing(8)`) below the top of the scroll
container. The discussion rail sticks there too, as tall as the window less
`--document-layout-aside-offset` (default `--spacing(24)`: the bar above and the offsets).
`DocumentLayoutAsideTab` is `{ value, label, count?, content }`.

## Accessibility

- The contents and the margin are two `aside` landmarks, named "Contents" and "Comments" (override
  with `aria-label`). Below their tier they are `display: none`, so they leave the accessibility
  tree with the screen.
- The aside is an `aside` landmark named by its `title` ("Comments", "On this page"); its tabs are
  a Base UI tab list named the same, and the × is a "Close" button. It is non-modal: focus stays
  where the reader puts it, and the page beside it keeps working. Below L the discussion is a
  modal bottom sheet with its own close.
- Source order is reading order: the title area, the contents, the text, what follows it, then
  the comments — whatever the columns look like.
- The skeleton is one `status` region named "Loading page" with `aria-busy`; its bars are hidden.

| Key                         | Action                                                                |
| --------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| <kbd>Tab</kbd>              | Move through the contents, the text's links, then the margin or aside |
| <kbd>←</kbd> / <kbd>→</kbd> | Move between the aside's tabs                                         |
| <kbd>Esc</kbd>              | Close the discussion sheet (below L)                                  |
